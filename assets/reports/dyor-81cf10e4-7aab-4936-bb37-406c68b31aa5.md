## 1. Research Question

What was the origin of European werewolf belief, what drove the werewolf trials of the 16th–17th centuries (e.g., Peter Stumpp 1589, Gilles Garnier 1573, the Jura/Franche-Comté cases of the 1520s–1600s, and Thiess of Kaltenbrun 1692), how did these trials relate to contemporaneous witch trials, and in what distinct senses—documented legal/historical events, natural phenomena misread (wolf attacks, serial killers, clinical lycanthropy, rabies, ergotism, porphyria, hypertrichosis), or supernatural shapeshifting—can werewolves be said to have been 'real'?

## 2. Scope

- **Population:** Individuals accused, tried, or confessing to werewolf transformation or association (including alleged werewolf-witches), and separately, documented wolf-attack victims and perpetrators later framed as werewolves (e.g., Beast of Gévaudan killings 1764-67); comparative reference to witch-trial defendants for demographic contrast (sex ratio, social status)
- **Geography:** Continental Europe, with focus on regions of documented trial clusters: Holy Roman Empire (esp. Rhineland, Bedburg), France (Jura, Franche-Comté, Gévaudan, Auvergne), Livonia (modern Estonia/Latvia), and earlier Mediterranean/Classical sources (Greece, Rome) and medieval French/Norse literary sources for belief origins; not bounded to a single modern nation-state
- **Period:** Classical antiquity (Ovid, Petronius, Herodotus, c. 1st century BCE–1st century CE) through medieval sources (Canon Episcopi 9th–10th c., Marie de France's Bisclavret c. 1200, Norse sagas), the core trial era (1520s–1692), the Beast of Gévaudan episode (1764-67), and modern cinematic reinterpretation (The Wolf Man, 1941) as a terminus for tracing how popular tropes formed

### Out of scope

- Non-European werewolf-analog traditions (e.g., Navajo skin-walkers, Japanese kitsune, African were-hyena beliefs) except where used for contrast if explicitly flagged
- Post-1941 film, television, and literary werewolf media beyond establishing that The Wolf Man (1941) is the source of silver-bullet/full-moon/bite-contagion tropes
- Modern werewolf subculture, fiction fandom, or neopagan practice
- General witch trial history except where directly comparative to werewolf trial incidence, geography, and sex ratio
- Unverifiable or anecdotal modern 'real werewolf' claims (e.g., contemporary hypertrichosis case reports used as clickbait) that cannot be checked against primary or peer-reviewed sources
- Veterinary or zoological study of actual wolf behavior and ecology except as relevant to assessing real wolf-attack hypotheses
- Detailed theological exegesis of the Canon Episcopi beyond its relevance to the shapeshifting-as-illusion doctrine

### Ambiguities the question recorded

- Whether 'werewolf trials' should be counted as a distinct legal category separate from witch trials in regions where lycanthropy charges were folded into broader witchcraft indictments, which would change trial counts and sex-ratio comparisons
- Whether the Beast of Gévaudan episode counts as a 'werewolf trial' phenomenon at all, given no individual was tried for lycanthropy, only hunted as a beast
- Whether clinical lycanthropy (a modern psychiatric diagnosis) can be responsibly retrojected onto early modern confessions without anachronism
- Whether 'documented' requires the underlying events (shapeshifting) to be true, or only that the trial/confession/execution itself is attested in primary sources

### Assumptions the question recorded

- Trial-related claims will be restricted to cases with surviving primary or well-sourced secondary documentation (e.g., Stumpp, Garnier, Jura/Franche-Comté records, Thiess of Kaltenbrun)
- Disease/medical hypotheses (rabies, ergotism, porphyria, hypertrichosis) will be treated as separately testable explanatory candidates for specific cases or symptom clusters, not as a unified theory of all werewolf belief
- The apparent male predominance among werewolf-trial defendants (versus predominantly female witch-trial defendants) will be treated as a pattern to be checked against regional trial statistics rather than assumed uniform across all of Europe
- Literary/mythological sources (Ovid's Lycaon, Petronius, Herodotus's Neuri, Norse ulfhednar/berserkers, Marie de France's Bisclavret, Canon Episcopi) will be used to trace the prehistory of belief, not as evidence of actual historical shapeshifting

## 3. Definitions

- Werewolf belief: the cultural/folkloric concept that a human can transform into a wolf, whether via supernatural means, demonic pact, curse, or psychological/physical affliction
- Werewolf trial: a legal or inquisitorial proceeding in which a person was accused, interrogated (often under torture), and sometimes executed specifically on charges of lycanthropy or wolf-transformation, as distinct from general witchcraft charges
- Clinical lycanthropy: a psychiatric syndrome in which a person believes themselves to be, or behaves as, an animal (specifically a wolf in this context), used here as one candidate 'natural cause' explanation
- Documented event: a historical occurrence substantiated by primary sources (trial records, chronicles, confessions, legal documents), independent of whether the content of the confession was factually true
- \'Real' (three senses to be kept separate): (1) supernatural shapeshifting as literally occurring, (2) the trials/executions/confessions/beliefs as genuine historical events regardless of the truth of the underlying claim, (3) natural phenomena (wolf attacks, disease, psychiatric or metabolic conditions) that may have been misread as lycanthropy
- Hounds of God motif: the Thiess of Kaltenbrun 1692 Livonian account of benevolent werewolves fighting the devil, treated as a distinct folkloric strand from the demonic-werewolf trials elsewhere in Europe
